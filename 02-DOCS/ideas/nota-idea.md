**Under One Roof** — Samuel, David and Raúl
*One plan. One home. Everyone in it.*

**What we're building**

A family's financial plan is full of numbers that are hard to feel. So we're turning it into something everyone understands: **their own home**. The foundations are their emergency fund, the roof is their protection, the walls are their debt, the plumbing is their monthly cash flow, and the garden is what they're growing for the future. One look, and they see what's solid and what needs work.

A calm voice walks them through the house and through the years ahead. And the part we care about most: **they can stop it at any moment and ask**, out loud or with a tap. The house turns to what they asked about and answers with their own plan. It ends with one clear first step, checks in with them on day 3 and when their plan's key dates come up, and gives their advisor a short note on what they asked, what worries them and what they decided.

**Why we think it works**

- **It grabs you.** A cracked house needs no explanation. Samantha scores 82, "strong", and her house looks perfect, until we move forward to age 73 and her garden dries up. That's the problem a good score can hide.
- **Anyone can use it.** When each person opens their own plan, they choose the mode that suits them. The first is much simpler, so that even older people can follow it and find their way: big buttons, the voice leads, and you hold a button to talk, like sending a voice note. The second is the complete one, with an explore mode that shows every figure.
- **It moves people to act.** Every repair is a step Sherpas already calculated, with the score before and after. You pick a date, and you see that part of your house get fixed.
- **It's really theirs.** Michael rents, and his 2027 house is being built next door. Samantha's house is paid off, and Ethan's room goes quiet when he leaves for college. In a couple, each person sees their own part. And we start by asking what worries them, something none of the four plans records today.
- **It's honest.** Every number comes from the plan. We never invent a future.

**What we left behind**

We explored several ideas before landing here:
- **A TV series of the plan** felt too passive and not serious enough.
- **A world to explore** looked great but would lose older users, so it became the house.
- **Two AI advisors debating** was too close to the chat Sherpas already has.
- **A game to split spare cash** and **a message from your future self** were fun, but they only covered part of the challenge or would have needed numbers we don't have.

**Why a phone, and what else we said no to**

- **A phone, because that's where families are.** The Weahs, with two jobs and two small kids, read their plan in short bursts on their phone. It opens from a link the advisor sends: nothing to install, no account to create. An app store download would lose many people, especially older ones, before they even start.
- **Not a desktop dashboard or a PDF.** Sherpas already gives families an online plan and a report. Another screen full of charts wouldn't change how they feel about it.
- **Not daily messages by chat or email.** They're useful for the days after, so we kept them only as short reminders on the plan's own dates, not as the main experience.
- **Not a live AI voice conversation for everything.** It's expensive with our shared credits and can be slow. The guided voice is prepared in advance, and the live part is kept for their questions.
- **Not reading emotions by camera or cloning family voices.** It would feel invasive. We adapt only to what each person chooses and says.
- **Not full 3D from day one.** We start with a clear 2D house that works on any phone.

**What could go wrong**

3D takes time, so we'll build the simple 2D version first and only add 3D if it works on a phone. And before the demo, we'll test it with a few people outside the team, including someone older who rarely uses apps.
